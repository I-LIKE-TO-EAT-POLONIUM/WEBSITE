Insurgency Group

Reference Document

A splinter faction descended from a WW2-era German unit cut off from its parent battalion in Russia.

Some info:

  • Origins trace to a WW2 German unit operating in Russia that lost communications with its main battalion and was never able to reestablish contact.
  • Went to ground rather than attempt a breakout back to friendly lines; evolved over generations into an independent organization.
  • Main Operations HQ is located in the Komi Republic, Russia.
  • Standard uniform pattern: Frog Skin camo.
  • Standard arms: AK-family rifles (AKM, AK-74, AKS-74U, RPK/RPK-74, PKM) plus SVD Dragunov.

Flag Patches:

Members may wear a flag patch of either their home country or their current operating country. No other flags permitted.

Unit Structure

  • Command — regional/theater level (e.g. Europe Command, MENA Command). Led by Colonel / Lieutenant Colonel + Command Sergeant (SEA).
  • Cell — sub-regional (country/large metro area). Led by Major + Master Sergeant (SEA).
  • Group — operational subdivision within a Cell. Led by Captain + Sergeant (SEA).
  • Unit — smallest operational element, 3-6 members. Led by Lieutenant + Lead Operative (SEA).

Chain of Command Example:

Commander / Lieutenant Commander
  → Colonel / Lieutenant Colonel (Command)
    → Major (Cell)
      → Captain (Group)
        → Lieutenant (Unit)
          → Fighters / Operatives / Recruits

Specialty Classification System

Every member is trained in Basic Rifle Combat. Specialties are additional qualifications layered on top, coded by class.

A-Class — Combative

  • A1: Rifleman
  • A2: Marksman
  • A3: Heavy Weapons
  • A4: Scout / Recon

B-Class — Support

  • B1: Medical
  • B2: Support (logistics/supply)
  • B3: Communications
  • B4: Engineering / Demolitions

C-Class — Development

  • C1: Training and Drills
  • C2: Intelligence

D-Class — Vehicles

  • D1: Driver / Transport
  • D2: Vehicle Gunner
  • D3: Vehicle Maintenance / Mechanic
  • D4: Convoy / Escort

E-Class — Specialized Detachments

Standing assignments to a specific, named detachment rather than a portable personal specialty.

  • E1: HQ Security Detachment
  • E2: Checkpoint / Escort Detachment
  • E3: Quick Reaction Force (QRF)
  • E4: Cyber Warfare
  • E5: Sabotage
  • E6: Infiltration / Deep Cover
  • E7: Translator — minimum 3 assigned per Outpost, matched to host country/region

Standard Arms

  • AKM — standard rank-and-file rifle
  • AK-74 — 5.45x39, favored where Warsaw Pact stockpiles are accessible
  • AKS-74U — shortened carbine, officer/Command PDW, vehicle crews, Scout/Recon
  • RPK / RPK-74 — squad automatic weapon, Heavy Weapons
  • PKM — general-purpose machine gun, Heavy Weapons
  • SVD Dragunov — designated Marksman rifle

Locations

Main Operations HQ — Komi Republic, Russia

  • Home base of the Commander and Lieutenant Commander.
  • 2 floors plus a simple basement — an administrative/command node, not a garrison.
  • Staffed additionally by Chief Officers (e.g. Chief Training and Drills Officer), personally handpicked by the Commander/Lieutenant Commander.
  • Access: off a rural dirt road, itself branching off two further dirt roads, which connect to a rural paved road a few miles out — a layered, low-visibility approach with multiple natural checkpoints.

1st floor:

Day-to-day operations, communications, admin, staff quarters/common areas.

2nd floor:

Commander / Lieutenant Commander offices, planning rooms, secure comms.

Basement:

Records/archives, secure storage, hardened comms/server room.

HQ Guard Detachment:

Supplied by Cell: Komi. One 9-person group on duty at a time, cycling with 2 other groups held at the nearby Komi Outpost.

Komi Outpost

  • Located approximately 41 miles from HQ.
  • 1,400 active personnel, 103 reserve.
  • Made up of 3 separate sites, all close together.
  • Commanded by its own Cell Command (Cell: Komi), under a Major + Master Sergeant (SEA).
  • Cell: Komi is responsible for supplying and running the HQ Security Detachment.

Founding Lore

In progress.

  • Origin: a German unit operating in Russia during WW2 that lost communications with its main battalion.
  • Splinter trigger: loss of contact (radio/courier chain broken, possible encirclement) with no way to reestablish it.
  • Rather than a forced breakout back to friendly lines, the unit went to ground and became self-sufficient, eventually settling in the remote Komi region.
  • Over generations, presumed to have shifted away from its original WW2 German identity through local recruitment and survival necessity — extent of any remaining connection still undecided.
